# iBrawls Web App

iBrawls is a React, Vite, Three.js browser game with a local Node/WebSocket relay for development and a Cloudflare Worker Durable Object relay for deployment.

## Local Development

1. Install root dependencies:
   `npm install`
2. Start the local app and relay:
   `npm run dev`
3. Open `http://localhost:3000`.

The root dev server runs `server.ts`, which hosts Vite in middleware mode and provides the local WebSocket matchmaking/gameplay relay.

Registered accounts reserve their saved Spartan Pilot Identity nameplate as a registered display name. The account owner keeps the plain base name in public identity surfaces. Guests or other signed-in accounts using the same registered base name are rendered by the Worker relay with a random session-only discriminator such as `ASpence501#1001`; the editable base name remains capped at 10 characters, but rendered collision names may be longer because of the suffix. Signed-in cloud-save updates that try to claim another account's registered display name are rejected and the UI reverts to the account's previous registered name.

Hosting a multiplayer match now creates a staged pre-match lobby instead of immediately starting gameplay. The host configures access (`open`, `private`, or `password`), game mode (`sandbox` shown as iBrawls, or `grifball`), map/custom map, player slot count, observer allowance, match timer, and kills/goals target through the shared `MatchLobbyConfig` contract in `src/network/protocol.ts`. Players can join staged lobbies by public room code, password prompt, or direct invite; the host starts the match with a `start_match` handshake and relays broadcast `match_start` with the authoritative config before clients enter gameplay.

Multiplayer rooms stay advertised while a staged or running match has an open player slot. The Global Broadcast player list and lobby browser show mode, map, access, slots, observers, timer, and target metadata through `MatchLobbySummary`; private lobbies do not expose joinable public room codes, and password lobbies expose only a `hasPassword` flag. Passwords are hashed server-side and are never sent in presence, lobby lists, invites, or sync payloads. Direct invites include relay-issued invite tokens for the target user so invited users can enter private/password rooms without public listing access. Quick Play only matches open, non-password lobbies with available player slots. The local relay and Worker relay both cap active players at 8 total (host + 7 guests), keep observers outside player-slot capacity, and assign stable spawn slots so simultaneous joins or respawns do not stack players on one spawn point. Signed-in accounts are limited to one active browser page/location at a time: a newer signed-in page displaces older lobby/gameplay sockets for the same account to prevent player cloning.

Multiplayer match loading uses the gameplay WebSocket roster, not the lobby presence id. The relays add optional hue/loadout identity and match visual model policy to participant entries, then forward `match_loading_status` sync packets with each browser's loading percentage, stage, and ready flag. `src/components/loading/useMatchLoadingGate.ts` keeps the local/remote loading roster including players and observers, holds HUD/chat/replay overlays behind `MatchLoadingOverlay`, renders participant previews through the active match policy, and releases gameplay only after every connected participant is ready or has crossed the loading timeout fallback.

## V3 Weapon Animation QA

V3 startup reuses identical armor vertex buffers across bot colors while keeping palette materials separate. Loading stages yield through a painted frame before synchronous scene construction, so the progress display can advance during setup. The first V3 character still builds its detailed geometry; additional bot hues reuse that work.

Single Player → Training Sandbox Setup → **Version 3 Preview** enables V3 player and bot models with the approved Blender animations in local training. V2 remains the default; tournaments and multiplayer retain their existing model restrictions. The training selection survives bot setup and respawning, and a previous multiplayer lobby cannot override it. Gameplay maps elapsed combat timers to authored attack phases, keeps distance-paced walking/running under weapon carry, attack, and recovery poses, holds the slide until movement ends and then plays its standing recovery, and follows a sword lunge with its lateral cut. Bodies and third-person weapon sockets share one sampled pose; first-person weapons use the same baked tracks in camera space. The V3 skull ball follows runner carry, jab and charged-throw poses while existing possession and projectile physics remain authoritative. Gameplay refinement and live Grifball validation remain separate work. See [animation release scope and known validation gaps](scripts/v3/blender/RELEASE_SCOPE.md).

V3 gameplay uses five Blender-baked crouch stances for unarmed, hammer, sword, pistol, and ball carry. Player input, bots, remote players, observer views, and recorded replay posture drive entry, breathing hold, and recovery without vertically scaling the model. Interrupted transitions reverse smoothly; weapon swaps retain posture. Crouch-walking solves the bent legs to moving foot targets, and attacks retain their combat timing and hand grips while crouched. Slides and sword lunges retain their dedicated animations. Multiplayer sync includes the slide flag to distinguish crouch-walking from sliding. ## Browser Performance

iBrawls expects hardware-accelerated WebGL. The app warns when WebGL is unavailable, blocked, or routed through a slow software rasterizer. Microsoft Edge remains supported, but if Edge reports accelerated WebGL while sustained gameplay stays below 20 FPS for 5 seconds, the app shows an Edge-specific degraded graphics-path warning with `edge://gpu`, `edge://settings/system`, Edge update, and GPU driver troubleshooting steps, then recommends Chrome or Firefox for best performance on that device.

## AI Systems

Tournament mode includes a **NeuralNet** bracket option that runs exported PPO browser brains for live 1v1 matches. `CombatDRV2` remains the default stable brain under `public/brains/combat_dr_v2/`, `CombatDRV4` is available under `public/brains/combat_dr_v4/` for live practice testing, and `CombatUpgradeV3` is selectable under `public/brains/combat_upgrade_v3/`. These browser artifacts (`manifest.json`, `weights.bin`, and `fixtures.json`) are generated with `python -m ibrawls_rl.export_browser_brain`. The v1 brains use combat mode, observation version 1, env spec v7, frame stack 4, decision interval 5, input width 560, and action factors `[9, 4, 4, 2, 2, 2]`; `CombatUpgradeV3` uses observation version 3, env spec v9, input width 688, and the same action factors.

`CombatUpgradeV3` is an anti-bait brain family, not a replacement for the shipped `CombatDRV2` artifact. Its training contract uses observation version 3 / env spec v9, appending `combat_threat_v3` threat features after the v2 pressure block so the policy can see ready melee opponents, range margins, closing speed, facing-self state, and passive-bait risk. Training can mix scripted bait curricula with `combat_bait_layout_mix`, `combat_bait_opponent`, and `combat_bait_reward_scale`, plus the `dangerApproach`, `baitDisengage`, `trapDeath`, and `missedAttackOpportunity` reward components. `missedAttackOpportunity` is the close-range discipline penalty for holding a ready weapon while a hostile is already inside the sim's immediate hit volume. The strongest scripted bait profile is `passive_bait_duelist`, which swaps into sword, holds just outside ready threat range, dances in the spacing band, and punishes recovery windows instead of charging a ready opponent. Promotion checks include the normal combat matrix, a frozen-snapshot matrix score, and the anti-bait matrix: `lone_wolf_score >= 0.75`, `frozen_snapshot_score >= 0.55`, `anti_bait_score >= 0.70`, and `trap_death_rate <= 0.20`. `CombatDRV2` stays the default selectable NeuralNet brain.

The reinforcement trainer and dashboard live under `python/ibrawls_rl/`. The TypeScript sim remains the gameplay source of truth while Python owns PPO training, frame-stacked short history, reward-component logging, combat evaluation matrices, and frozen snapshot league grades. Combat training can use explicit scenario mixes such as `1v1x16`, `1v2x6`, `1v3x6`, `1v7x2`, `ffa4x6`, and `ffa8x4`, with a lone-wolf reward scale for singleton teams in asymmetric layouts; combat mode does not add a low-health scenario, which remains a Grifball runner condition. The sim action schema includes nearest-hostile aim for combat policies, optional reward-discipline penalties expose wasted attack/dash/jump/swap/repeated-action behavior as `reward_component/*` metrics in the control board, and older 21-logit combat checkpoints auto-migrate into the 22-logit action head when warm-starting if network width/depth still match.

RL runs also write `training_metadata.json` with the model contract and sampled mechanics coverage. The Control Board Evaluate tab reads that Brain Contract for selected models, falls back to partial `config_used.toml` data for older runs, and can run a combat Mechanics suite that repeats the matrix over nominal, low-band, high-band, and live-current mechanics so rankings account for worst-preset robustness. **Custom AI Behavior** (the `custom` difficulty, formerly "Custom Matrix Override") exposes *every* engine-wired AI dial in a grouped panel: base neural-matrix knobs (reflex latency, anticipation, strafe/evade, weapon-swap IQ, playstyle, weapon prioritization) plus advanced behavior overrides — `aiSpatialIQ`, `aiFeintChance`, `aiPressureAggression`, `aiSpacingBand` (combat spacing), and `aiSkipPressure` (disable post-hit pressure chaining). > Convention: any new AI-behavior knob added to the combat engine must also be exposed in the Custom AI Behavior panel, persisted in `AITuning` presets, and threaded through `RosterSlotConfig`.

**PRESSURING state:** When a bot lands a non-lethal hit and `pressureAggression` is above threshold (passive bots skip), it enters `PRESSURING` instead of retreating through a full `COOLDOWN`. The bot closes faster, uses shorter attack timers, prefers hammer re-swings or sword lunges, and exits when the target dies, becomes invulnerable, leaves range, or the pressure timer expires. Chain length scales with score context (longer when behind, shorter when protecting a large lead).

**Match state awareness:** `deriveMatchStateMultipliers()` reads `scorePlayer` / `scoreEnemy` (and tournament `killsToWin`) to modulate aggression, spacing, cooldowns, weapon-swap IQ, coin-flip trade avoidance, and PRESSURING duration. Large leads play safer; large deficits press harder; scores within 2 get peak tactical IQ; match point behavior splits between extreme commit (aggressive playstyle) and extreme patience (passive playstyle).

**Weapon combo strings (IQ ≥ 70):** High weapon-swap IQ bots chain mid-combat sequences stored in `aiMatchContext.comboState`. **Mixup** (hammer hit → sword lunge), **Safe Finish** (double hammer), **Bait & Smash** (IQ ≥ 90: sword flash → hammer punish), and **Double Tap** (sword hit → hammer finish) respect swap lockouts, abort on target state changes, and override tactical swaps while active. Mixup Artist tournament/sandbox opponents initiate combos most often.

**Feinting (Hard+/Custom IQ ≥ 60):** Bots with non-zero `feintChance` can abort forward approaches, flash a sword swap before reverting to hammer, sideways-dash out of committed charges when the opponent swings, or rush in without lunging. Each feint respects a 3–5s per-bot cooldown stored in `aiMatchContext`. Counter-heavy players (high `counterRate` in the adaptive model) see reduced feint pressure.

**Smart evasion:** Incoming sword lunges trigger perpendicular dashes (or hammer jumps / sidestep jumps as fallbacks) using `aiSpatialStrategy` to pick a side away from the arena boundary and informed by learned dodge bias. Detection range jitters ±20% with `spatialIQ`; bait dodges can fire near 12m when an opponent holds sword. After a successful evasive dash, bots may enter `CHARGE_ATTACK` when the target is recovering and in range. Learned opponent `reactionTime` slightly scales evasion trigger distance.

**Arena spatial control:** `scorePosition` and `getSpatialMovementBias` (gated by `spatialIQ`) steer `APPROACHING` and `SIDE_STEPPING` movement: bots recentre when exposed on the edge, cut off retreat paths when targets are pinned, and press harder when an opponent is cornered. `SPAWN_GUARDING` uses a corridor-aware aim angle and recentres when too close to the boundary. Hard+ target selection adds a bonus for edge-pinned opponents.

**Locomotion mechanics (sprint & slide):** `aiMovementMechanics.ts` lets bots use the same optional movement mechanics the player tunes in Gameplay / Mechanics Options. When `enableSprint` is on, bots sprint (scaled by `speedSprint`) to close ground while `APPROACHING`/`DANCING_FORWARD`/`PRESSURING` or to chase a fleeing target. When `enableSlide` is on, bots commit a `slideDistance`/`speedSlide` slide as a mid-range gap-closer and then respect `slideCooldown`. Both read live from settings each frame—toggling a mechanic off or retuning its speed/distance/cooldown takes effect immediately—and never stack with dashes. Sprint/slide also drive the bot’s crouch posture and run/slide animation.

**Psychological pressure (Hard+, aggression ≥ 15):** After a lethal hit, bots rush the victim’s anticipated spawn (`aiPsychologicalPressure` + `aiMatchContext.psychState`) instead of resetting—lunge kills hold sword at the spawn lip as a telegraph. During neutral exchanges, tempo alternation toggles effective `reactionLatency` every ~9s (slow vs fast bands), and mid-range standoff timers in `SIDE_STEPPING` escalate commit chance using match-state multipliers from `aiTuning`.

**Multi-bot coordination (Hard+ offline):** When one bot tags damage, `aiBotCoordinator` sets a shared priority target for ~8s so allies focus fire. Two or more bots on the same target pincer with lateral offsets; attack commits stagger by role (pressure → flanker → punisher). Three or more assign punisher bots that wait for recovery windows before swinging. Easy difficulty skips coordination.

**Dynamic skill calibration (Normal/Hard/Nightmare):** `aiSkillCalibration` maintains a rolling window of the last 10 engagements per bot (kills, deaths, dodge/counter outcomes, time-between-deaths). When the player dominates, bots receive a subtle buff to reaction speed, anticipation, and lunge aggression; when the bot dominates, those knobs drift down slightly (±12.5% max). Disabled for Custom difficulty (including tournament opponents with explicit tuning) and Easy mode.

## Theater Mode

iBrawls includes a high-fidelity 3D "Theater Mode" archive that allows players to save, rename, search, filter, and play back their recorded gameplay sessions with granular media player control.

- **Fidelity Playback**: Instead of video recording, the system transcribes active gameplay structural snapshots at a stable **20Hz (50ms)** tick rate.
- **Zero-Movement Compression**: Drastically reduces file sizes (under ~200KB for an 8-minute 8-player match) by filtering out stationary pilot frames where coordinates change $<0.001$ and state matches the previous frame. Missing keyframe deltas are dynamically reconstructed on-the-fly during playback using reverse scanning.
- **Interpolation Engine**: Angles are resolved using shortest-path wrapping:
  $$\Delta\theta = \text{atan2}(\sin(\theta_B - \theta_A), \cos(\theta_B - \theta_A))$$
  to prevent visual spin flips when yaw bounds are crossed. Position coordinates are interpolated using linear LERP, presenting a fluid 60FPS spectator experience.
- **Full Joint & Skeletal Animations**: Spartan models dynamically run walk/run/sprint leg cycles, crouch/slide leaning, and weapon swinging/melee sweeping torso twists during playback by executing the full `animateSpartanModel` skeletal animation engine in the replay loop.
- **Swappable Attack Animation Presets**: `hammerAttackAnimation` and `swordAttackAnimation` can switch from the legacy weapon poses to programmatic high-fidelity hammer swing/melee and sword lunge/slash poses that use staged windups, stronger silhouettes, and recovery settling for clearer combat reads.
- **Gravity Hammer Slam Timing**: Gameplay / Mechanics Options exposes `hammerSlamWindupTime`, `hammerSlamAttackTime`, and `hammerSlamTimingLocked`; locked edits preserve the legacy 0.28s / 0.12s phase ratio while unlocked edits tune windup and strike phases independently. Damage and VFX land after `windup + attack`, while hammer recovery and melee sliders remain separate.
- **Replicated VFX and Audio Cues**: Dashing, lunging, and weapon swings automatically trigger their matching sound effects, speed lines trails, evasion box particles, and colossal hammer ground-impact splash explosions, shockwaves, and ground burn decals.
- **Replay Heatmaps**: New replay files include explicit `heatmap` event data (`version: 1`) for kill, death, and recorded medal events with replay time, actor/victim ids, team, weapon, and world `x/z` location. Theater cards render an end-of-film 2D top-down heatmap preview when that data exists; the heatmap uses the map's top-down floor art as the base image, including the default Circular Arena/Hangar tile atlas plus authored custom floor tiles, obstacles, goal plates, and spawn markers when map data is available, so event positions line up with recognizable field geometry. The 3D replay view starts with a collapsible, resizable heatmap panel, and Theater can open a heatmap-only playback mode with the same timeline. Older replay files without `heatmap.events` show an empty/disabled heatmap instead of deriving events from frame counter deltas.
- **Replay Visual Policy**: New replay files store the match visual model policy and sanitized loadout-like metadata for playback. Older replay files with no visual policy keep legacy Version 1 playback visuals, and upload sanitization strips raw mesh/private reference data before replay metadata leaves the local archive.
- **Rolling Match Cache**: Features a local rolling cache of the last **5 auto-saved matches** (via IndexedDB) that overwrite sequentially. Players can commit cache items permanently to the **Replays Archive** with custom titles and descriptions.
- **Spectator Controls**: A bottom glassmorphism timeline control bar enables:
  - Timeline scrubbing/seeking to any second of the match.
  - Variable speed multiplier rates (`0.25x`, `0.5x`, `1.0x`, `2.0x`, `4.0x`).
  - Active lock-target tracking dropdown to focus first-person, third-person orbit, or free fly-camera on any recorded Spartan.
  - Fully simulated scoreboard and SFX/sparks triggers synced forward-only to prevent audio cluster when scrubbing.

If the normal challenger misses that deadline and another can meet it, the timely defender takes over. Viable assignments remain stable, and one last defender stays reserved.

Defense also tracks actual carrier displacement. A carrier that stays within half a world unit for the full Defense lookahead period expires the predicted movement: the interceptor pressures the carrier's current position while preserving safety and containment rules. Brief combat pauses retain the cut, and observed movement restores prediction. Ownership, round and match changes, loose possession, paused play or disabled defense clear this history. Human and neural carriers are observed without receiving tactical orders.

When visible motion is obstructed and defense falls back to a goalward route, intermediate interception samples follow that route at the carrier's predicted speed. They do not cut across obstacle corners along the straight line to the forecast endpoint. Unobstructed observed velocity retains its existing prediction.

Passing compares throw speeds using the existing charge, bounce, arena-boundary and settling rules. The ball remains uncatchable in flight. A pass needs goal progress, a reachable scripted receiver, time to charge, and safe opponent pickup/attack margins, including nearby respawns. Receivers move toward the recovery point; the passer holds to charge and checks the live opportunity before release. Receiver death, changed ownership, disabled throwing or disabled tactics cancels the intent. Humans and neural players remain observed; they are not assigned pass/receive orders. The Defense / passing tab includes independent toggles, pressure, safety, containment and passing-risk controls, advanced settings, interception markers, throw paths and candidate rejection reasons.

Styles applies persistent team preferences for aggression, patience, formation width, support distance and runner risk over the other module settings. Balanced, Patient support and Aggressive pressure shortcuts change those five preferences; all fields support team overrides, preset storage, module reset and live tuning. Seeded variation chooses only similarly useful eligible runner routes and tank engagements; it keeps the same preference during a possession and respects commitment, progress timeouts, pass viability and goal emergencies. Tactical difficulty uses the mean scripted roster level, including respawning bots: easy teams refresh more slowly and use shorter carrier projections, while hard/nightmare teams refresh more often. It does not change speed, damage, health, reach or cooldowns, and custom difficulty uses normal tactical timing.

Adaptation observes nearby opponents' approach speed and width relative to the play, sampling visible motion at most twice per second. It stores up to 24 observations and requires both a minimum count and a consistency-based confidence threshold. The default needs at least nine consistent samples (about 4.5 seconds); uncertain observations keep the configured style. Confident compact approaches widen the formation; fast pressure brings support closer and makes the runner more cautious. Adaptation strength bounds the response. Evidence expires after four seconds without eligible movement and clears on a new round, match reset, roster identity change or module disable. It survives ordinary possession changes and deaths. The inspector separates configured preferences from effective preferences and shows evidence, confidence, observed tendencies, refresh timing and active adaptation. Advanced confidence/evidence controls are collapsed. Disable learning to keep the chosen base style, or disable Styles to return to the module settings and existing combat difficulty behavior.

Objective movement follows checked ground routes around custom-map obstacles and stops explicitly when no route exists. With the space model enabled, ball recovery chooses a reachable retriever by route distance; a blocked incumbent or human approach cannot prevent a reachable teammate from recovering the ball. Static navigation grids are cached separately from moving threats; thin-wall segment checks and rounded combatant clearance match the current axis-aligned collision rules. Goal plates that are non-collidable stay walkable. This is ground navigation: coarse-grid narrow passages and jump-only platforms may be rejected, and airborne attacks remain approximate. Escort arrival and risk estimates do not guarantee a won engagement. Ball recovery predictions run the existing physics at 60 Hz; variable live timesteps and moving spawn exclusions can shift predicted timing and endpoints. Live plans refresh and pass release checks current participants again.

### Grifball validation suite (VS5: correctness, parity and acceptance gates)

The suite freezes a manifest and executes scenario or autonomous-match jobs through the real browser gameplay loop. The CLI runs the profile's Node checks, TypeScript, production client/relay builds, an explicit validation build, and a production startup smoke before running the serial job queue. It uses project-pinned Playwright 1.63.0 and its matching Chromium, with a fresh offline browser context for every job. Install the browser once after `npm ci` (and again when its pin changes):

```sh
npm run validate:grifball:install
npm run validate:grifball -- --suite quick
npm run validate:grifball -- --suite standard --dry-run
npm run validate:grifball -- --suite quick --dry-run --write-manifest output/grifball-validation/quick-manifest.json
npm run validate:grifball -- --manifest output/grifball-validation/quick-manifest.json --out output/grifball-validation/my-run
npm run validate:grifball -- --resume output/grifball-validation/my-run --out output/grifball-validation/resumed-run
npm run validate:grifball -- --resume output/grifball-validation/my-run --case goal-quick --out output/grifball-validation/goal-rerun
npm run test:grifball-validation
npm run test:grifball-driver
```

The runner owns a loopback server on **port 3000**. Stop your development server before running; an occupied port is reused only when its build/runtime identity matches exactly. The runner never kills an unrelated server. Chromium needs a working WebGL environment; a failed renderer produces failed evidence. Linux hosts may also need Playwright's documented browser system dependencies. Ctrl+C retains completed results and closes owned resources.

Artifacts default to a new `output/grifball-validation/<run-id>/` directory: `report.html`, `summary.json`, the manifest and frozen plan, build/runtime identity, per-job fixtures/seeds/traces/metrics and structured failure snapshots, check/browser logs, built applications, and startup/arena/failure screenshots. Existing output directories are rejected. Results are flushed and published atomically after each job; an interrupted job restarts from tick zero. Resume verifies the complete manifest/plan, scoped source changes, dependency lockfile, built assets, Node/platform/architecture, Playwright, and Chromium version/executable before copying completed evidence into a new directory. Previous failed attempts stay visible and cannot become a clean pass. Use `--resume ... --case ...` to rerun a case with its history; jobs outside that case remain not run. A changed build requires a new run without `--resume`.

Quick contains 4 scenarios and 32 paired jobs (160 simulated seconds maximum). Acceptance uses `--protocol path/to/protocol.json` alongside an authored manifest. Save the protocol **before** the run; it is copied into artifacts and must match on resume. An example structure follows (replace the calibration values with a reviewed serial pilot):

```json
{
  "version": 1,
  "quality": { "tuningSeeds": [1, 2, 3], "tuningMapIds": ["tuning-map"] },
  "performance": {
    "calibrationId": "reviewed-pilot-id",
    "environmentHash": "COPY_THE_64_CHARACTER_HASH_FROM_THE_PILOT",
    "controlledSerial": true,
    "maxBaselineRatio": 1.2,
    "maxNoiseRatio": 3,
    "minRefreshSamples": 20
  }
}
```

The calibration ID must match the manifest, and the environment hash must match the running machine/browser. The manifest freezes the absolute planner p95 budget; the protocol freezes the candidate/baseline ratio and allowed max/median noise ratio. Insufficient samples, excessive noise, missing calibration or another environment yield **inconclusive**, with measured budget violations still retained. `controlledSerial` is an operator declaration: stop other builds, simulations and browser batches during measurement. No universal 4 ms target is silently enabled.

Gate protocol **version 2** offers an explicit alternative for varying planner workloads: add `performance.noise: { "method": "aligned-replay-windows", "windowRefreshes": 8 }` and use a new calibration ID frozen before execution. The CLI repeats every baseline and candidate job separately, stores those trials under `performance-replay/`, and previews the extra job/time budget. Exact gameplay and refresh-tick agreement are required. Fixed consecutive windows sum the same refreshes in both runs; every window, including the final partial window, must meet `maxNoiseRatio`. No outliers are removed. Missing/truncated traces, zero-duration windows, changed outcomes or incompatible evidence are inconclusive. Both passes must meet the original absolute and paired p95 budgets. Version 1 retains its original max/median rule; historical results are not reclassified. This mode requires fresh controlled browser evidence and does not itself establish M7 performance acceptance.

Validation job JSON also includes bounded `decisions` diagnostics: initial and two-second samples plus possession, ball-state, phase and death/respawn transitions. Each sample records actor positions/velocities, health, weapon/FSM state, planned/pressure/last-lunge target IDs, role/reason, destination, route status and runner/defense/tanking modes. These are post-frame observations; recorded targets may belong to a previous commitment, and this sampling is not a complete attack log. A job keeps at most 64 samples (opening eight plus recent context) with an explicit dropped count. Browser reports share a 128-sample budget across jobs; full per-job CLI artifacts retain their own samples. Older reports may omit this additive diagnostic field. These samples do not change gameplay hashes or substitute for acceptance checks.

Quality requires full matches on at least two distinct maps, paired seeds, both sides, all three styles, and a tuning ledger disjoint from held-out seeds/maps. The predeclared primary outcome is goal-margin delta with a minimum effect and seed count. Both sides are averaged within each case/opponent/seed; case/opponent strata have equal weight; confidence intervals use independent seed clusters (Student-t, 95%). Protocol `paired-seed-t95-v2` requires the pooled lower bound to exceed the minimum effect **and every declared case/opponent lower bound to exceed zero**. A confirmed regression beyond the minimum effect in any stratum fails; flat or uncertain strata remain inconclusive, with their case/map/opponent named in the report. Pooled wins cannot hide a failing matchup. Per-map/opponent supporting measures include completed-possession rates, exposure, coverage, role churn and stuck time. Missing opportunities, incomplete pairs, insufficient seeds and any capped/failed/missing match prevent a quality pass. Completion uses **all scheduled matches** as its denominator. A null comparison cannot claim improvement. Human review stays separate.

Matches start at authored spawns with neutral ball, fresh scores/health/cooldowns/memory and the normal countdown. They stop at the actual goal-target `matchEnd`. Reaching the simulation cap is an unfinished match, never a draw; Grifball does not currently end when its displayed offline clock reaches zero. Trials check frozen settings/roster ownership, meshes, finite state, ball ownership, health and legal pickup/scoring transitions. An external Node watchdog catches even a blocked or crashed renderer. Simulation timing is diagnostic unless the separate calibrated performance gate has sufficient compatible evidence.

## Physics & Collisions

To prevent players and AI characters from passing straight through one another, iBrawls incorporates a 2.5D cylinder-based rigid-body collision system:

- **Entity Cylinders**: Every active, living participant (local player, main AI, and custom bots/remote players) is bounded by a model-profile collision cylinder. V2 Medium keeps the legacy radius of **0.55m** with **1.8m** standing / **1.2m** crouching height, while V2 Large uses a broader **0.75m** radius with **2.2m** standing / **1.45m** crouching height. Melee, hammer-impact, and sword-lunge target checks add the same Large radius bonus so the larger visual body has a matching gameplay hitbox.
- **Kinematic Resolution**: When two participants overlap both horizontally and vertically, they are pushed apart by **50%** of the overlap depth each along the collision normal.
- **Velocity Normal Damping**: To ensure collisions feel solid and prevent jittering or high-speed passthroughs, the relative velocity component along the collision normal is cancelled when entities are moving towards each other.
- **Multi-iteration Solver**: The collision engine runs for **3 iterations** each frame inside `enforceArenaBounds` before bounding players to the circular arena, ensuring perfectly stable physics even in crowded multi-bot pincers.
- **Arena Boundary Walls**: Circular and rectangular arena bounds act as hard walls for live combatants and the Grifball ball. Free-ball physics receives the active map bounds in both the browser runtime and the headless sim, clamps the ball center by its radius, and reflects thrown-ball horizontal velocity so wall bounces are visible in both live play and the throw trajectory preview.
- **Zero-lag Rendering**: State positions are proactively synchronized to Three.js group meshes immediately following collision resolution to eliminate 1-frame rendering lag.

Armor fit validation now checks the regenerated placement contract beyond simple containment: full glove envelopes include palm plus finger-chain capsules, every armor slot must stay close to its authoritative source dimensions, paired armor must remain on the correct left/right side with mirrored centers, chest/back plates must keep front-rear ordering, exported resolved placements must preserve scale and rotation, non-regenerated slots must retain generated Mesh2Motion orientation, and regenerated arm armor long axes must align to the mannequin arm and finger chains. Partial-length thigh and shin source pieces are validated against radial mannequin coverage and center alignment rather than forcing them to stretch to the full joint-pair capsule height.

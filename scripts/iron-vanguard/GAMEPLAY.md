# Iron Vanguard gameplay integration

Select **Model Set → Version 3 Iron Vanguard** in local, tournament or multiplayer setup. This shared policy selects Iron Vanguard for the local player, main AI, all additional bots, remote players, loading previews and replay participants. Bot spawn and hue rebuild callbacks always use the current match policy. V1/V2 restore their original models even when an Iron Vanguard Armory loadout is saved. The Armory can preview Iron Vanguard separately.

`resolveLoadoutForVisualPolicy` marks V3 gameplay loadouts with `characterAsset: 'iron-vanguard'`; the rendering adapter then owns the imported skeleton and animation. Low-level legacy V3 builders remain available to prototype authoring tools. Iron Vanguard currently uses the existing V2-compatible attachment/visibility contract, standard ball visuals, the imported sword and a provisional pistol; prototype V3 animation, voxel-death and geometry-quality code must not be applied to it. Instances do not rebuild when only prototype quality settings change.

## Runtime asset

- Approved source: `output/iron-vanguard/iron-vanguard-hammer-v018-shoulder-corrective.casc`.
- Export/clean workflow: [Cascadeur browser export](cascadeur/README.md#local-browser-swing-test).
- Build the shipping copy with `powershell -File scripts/iron-vanguard/build_gameplay_asset.ps1`.
- Output: `public/assets/iron-vanguard/hammer-v018.glb`, loaded lazily once with Three's Meshopt decoder.
- Geometry: 23,726 body triangles + 16,707 hammer triangles (40,433 total), 6.11 MB download. The unsimplified review asset remains separate.
- Rig: 61 bones, one 70-frame/30-fps clip, 70 per-frame shoulder correctives; the final frame reuses the first correction.
- Instances share geometry and animation data, with independent skeletons, materials and playback. Removal cancels pending attachment and disposes instance resources. Team paint excludes the hammer.

The model is floor-aligned at roughly 1.8 m body height, facing -Z. Its authored hammer remains on the exported skeleton so hand contacts and correctives sample the same source frame. Adapter groups provide existing weapon/effect attachment points without reparenting the skeleton.

## Animation and gameplay

The combat state machine still owns damage, cooldowns and movement. Wind-up maps source frames 0–24; strike 24–34; post-impact reload 34–70. Follow-through receives 1/11 of reload, preserving the accepted 0.10 s / 1.00 s split. Match timings can change without changing the hit's source pose. Saved settings are 0.30 s wind-up, 0.20 s strike, and 1.10 s reload: 1.60 s total. No global settings are changed by equipping the character.

The first-person hammer hides the body and displays the imported hammer; the sword view renders skinned arms and the imported sword. Third-person, remote roster and replay paths use the full character. Quick melee reuses the authored clip with its impact aligned to the melee event. Movement uses a separate library of 20 Blender-baked clips: idle breathing, forward/backward/left/right walk, run and crouch-walk, crouch, jump, fall, land, slide entry, slide hold, and slide exit. Short transitions blend clip changes; cadence follows speed and direction follows actor yaw. Adjacent directional gaits blend continuously for diagonal travel. Crouching bends the skeleton instead of applying the legacy vertical squash. Jump flags pass through local, bot, remote and replay rendering, with a brief apex grace period for older records without a flag. Sword uses the approved idle and the procedural attack/first-person arm layer described below. Dedicated pistol/ball actions, first-person hammer hands, richer materials and additional LODs remain future art work. The user-approved remaining shoulder folds are preserved.

## Jumping and moving landings

`ironVanguardAirborne.ts` shapes the baked jump/fall pose using smoothed horizontal velocity in the visual body's frame. The takeoff lead leg follows lateral travel or the preceding gait phase; forward/backward travel, strafing, and diagonals produce distinct leg reach. Speed controls the strength of the pose, descent extends the legs, and lateral limits keep the boots on their own sides. The gameplay root and vertical trajectory remain simulation-owned. The same rigid hip/hammer correction preserves both grips, and neither locomotion cadence nor looking retimes attacks or shoulder correctives.

Moving landings blend into the current walk/run/crouched gait over 0.08 seconds and retain a reduced pelvis impact dip during the 0.267-second land clip. A reachable lead-boot contact is taken from the blended pose, then releases within 0.09 seconds (earlier if travel or reach requires it), with a short lift into the next step. Stationary landings retain the authored absorption. Jump, slide, death, and teleport interruptions clear the old contact; stopping and reversal continue through the shared movement state machine. Contacts assume a level floor and do not provide terrain sampling or perfectly planted subsequent strides.

Choose **Jump While Moving** (`/iron-vanguard-animation-test.html?clip=jump-moving`) to review real translation through takeoff, ascent, apex, descent, and landing with eight travel directions, speeds from 0 to 8 m/s, crouched recovery, independent yaw/pitch, hammer attacks, and stop/reverse/turn changes after touchdown. The preview's example ballistic trajectory is independent of match jump settings; runtime poses use the supplied airborne flag and actual velocity. The source GLB and baked movement clips are preserved.

## Looking and body heading

`ironVanguardFacing.ts` adds a visual heading frame beneath the actor root. The actor yaw, world velocity, simulation, and attack clock remain authoritative. Moving gaits sample travel relative to the visual body heading; held-slide legs align with travel independently of looking, then gather toward facing as recovery rises. Small standing looks leave the feet still. Once the yaw difference exceeds 36°, a pair of 0.24-second steps turns the stance by up to 60° with alternating world support contacts. Moving, sliding, jumping, death, or teleporting clears those contacts; a stopped gait settles before starting a turn.

Yaw distributes across three spine bones (60° maximum) with up to 30° additional neck turn. Pitch is limited to ±60° and shared equally between chest and neck. The independent hammer root receives each chest transform, preserving the source two-hand offsets. At extreme rearward slide aim, the pose remains within these limits rather than matching the camera all the way behind; this is a known visual limitation, not a gameplay aim restriction. Shoulder morphs still sample the accepted attack clock. Existing roster pitch, observer host/client pitch and replay pitch now feed the shared animator, including AI combatants when they supply pitch; missing pitch remains level.

Choose **Look While Moving / Turn in Place** (`?clip=look-moving`) for independent yaw/pitch, eight world travel directions, crouched movement and a look sweep. Zero travel speed exercises turning steps. The same look controls are available in **Slide While Moving**, including attack and stand-up combinations. This pass has numerical/contact and screenshot checks; terrain adaptation, gait stride matching, and final artistic approval remain outstanding.

## Movement source and review

Open [the local movement review](http://localhost:3000/iron-vanguard-animation-test.html) to select, pause, scrub and orbit every clip. Choose **Slide Sequence** (or `?clip=slide-sequence`) to review the 0.40-second entry, a 1.20-second demonstration hold, the 0.65-second stand-up, and a brief ready pose. Choose **Slide While Moving** (or `?clip=slide-moving`) to test the shared gameplay state machine with actual translation across the floor. The camera follows the actor. Controls cover eight travel directions, 0–8 m/s, and continuing, stopping, reversing, or turning during stand-up. Scrubbing replays the movement state in small simulation steps; this mode does not force baked preview clips.

Gameplay holds the slide for as long as its slide flag remains active. Stationary recovery retains the 0.65-second authored timing; moving recovery accelerates up to 2x (about 0.40 seconds at 4 m/s, 0.33 seconds at 6+ m/s) without changing simulation speed or combat timing. Directional gait poses begin blending during the rise, including diagonals and crouched recovery. The right support boot briefly stays at its world contact, then lifts and releases before overextension. Stops and reversals ease the gait blend; jumps, death, teleports, and retriggered slides clear the old contact. This is a bounded push-off correction, not terrain-aware or fully planted locomotion; later gait stride matching still needs visual polish. Releasing during entry or retriggering during exit starts the next phase at the current depth; jumping interrupts it. The hammer checkbox combines locomotion with the accepted swing on its independent 1.60-second clock, with a separate hammer scrub control. Playback speed scales the whole preview; changing movement clips does not change attack duration. This is a first pass awaiting artist review, not final animation approval. The older approved Blender idle actions remain untouched; the new runtime idle starts from the later accepted Cascadeur ready pose.

The editable candidate is `output/iron-vanguard/movement-work/v008/movement.blend`. `scripts/iron-vanguard/blender/author_movement.py` bakes 60-fps local curves on the v018 transfer skeleton with fixed-length leg IK, reachable foot targets, and a shared pelvis/weapon transform. The low combat slide is a replacement authored by `author_slide.py`: left boot leading, right leg folded underneath the pelvis, and a close two-handed carry. Separate entry and stand-up actions shift weight, extend/gather the leading foot, and compress/extend the knees. Baked foot and knee targets keep the knee bend facing forward. During a slide attack, the pelvis and local leg offsets stay anchored to the slide while the upper body continues the accepted combat curves. The same pelvis correction applies to the independent hammer root, preserving both grips. All other movement clips retain the v004 source. Use a new output directory for each revision:

```powershell
& 'F:\Tools\Blender Foundation\Blender 5.2\blender.exe' --background --factory-startup --python-exit-code 1 --python scripts/iron-vanguard/blender/author_movement.py -- --output output/iron-vanguard/movement-work/NEW-REVISION
node scripts/iron-vanguard/pack_movement.mjs output/iron-vanguard/movement-work/NEW-REVISION

# Revise only the slide, preserving the other source clips.
& 'F:\Tools\Blender Foundation\Blender 5.2\blender.exe' --background --python-exit-code 1 --python scripts/iron-vanguard/blender/author_slide.py -- --base output/iron-vanguard/movement-work/v004 --output output/iron-vanguard/movement-work/NEW-SLIDE
node scripts/iron-vanguard/pack_movement.mjs output/iron-vanguard/movement-work/NEW-SLIDE
```

The packer generates `ironVanguardMovement.generated.ts` (about 881 KB before bundling/compression); no geometry is duplicated and `hammer-v018.glb` is unchanged. At rest with neutral look and no turn step, native attack poses and corrective weights are exact. During movement the lower-body curves replace the leg pose, while the same rigid motion applies to the hips and independent hammer root to retain grip contact. Foot target curves drive a two-bone leg correction during moving attacks so the native pelvis tilt does not push the feet through the floor. The shoulder correction remains tied to the native attack clock.

## Verification

```powershell
node --import tsx --test src/components/iron-vanguard/ironVanguardModel.test.ts src/components/iron-vanguard/ironVanguardMovement.test.ts src/components/iron-vanguard/ironVanguardFacing.test.ts src/components/iron-vanguard/ironVanguardAirborne.test.ts src/components/iron-vanguard/ironVanguardPolicy.test.ts src/model/modelSystem.test.ts src/model/modelVisualPolicy.test.ts src/game/ironVanguardTiming.test.ts src/tools/ironVanguardSwingTiming.test.ts
```

Tests read the shipping GLB and check its budget, skeleton, morphs, isolated instances, cleanup, pose reset after locomotion, combat impact synchronization, and loadout identity. Inspect ready/wind-up/impact/follow-through/recovery in the live renderer after any export changes. Movement tests also cover slide phase ordering, partial release/retrigger and jump interruption, exact phase endpoints, sampled mesh floor clearance, stable slide pelvis/knees through the full attack cycle, loop closure, leg segment lengths, sampled ankle height, grip stability across clips and moving attacks, reset without drift, direction selection, crouch scale and airborne/landing transitions, and slide entry/exit grip and floor clearance. Actual-travel tests additionally cover all eight directions at multiple speeds and actor yaws, bounded support-foot slip, early gait blending, root-motion independence, stopping, reversal, turning, jump/retrigger/death/teleport interruption, and hammer grip preservation throughout recovery. These do not establish complete mesh collision freedom, slope handling, perfect gait stride matching, or final artistic quality. Full-resolution native mesh comparisons from the review pipeline cannot be applied vertex-for-vertex to a simplified mesh.

Facing tests cover pitch through the attack cycle, 360° look wrapping during travel-aligned slides, stationary look versus alternating turn contacts, interruption cleanup, and combined attacks with walking, running, crouching and turning. Roster tests verify supplied pitch reaches every bot including main AI.

Airborne tests exercise 80 combinations of speed, travel direction, and attack state with sweeping yaw/pitch; they check root-motion independence, fixed leg lengths, floor clearance, both grips, correction timing, and jump/fall/land ordering. Additional checks verify directional leg reach, early landing strides, bounded contact slip, stationary impact absorption, and interruption cleanup. Browser review is still required for animation quality.

## Sword runtime

The approved `IV_Sword_Idle` action in `output/iron-vanguard/master-v021/iron-vanguard-master.blend` supplies upper-body breathing, the one-handed hold and finger rotations. The source file/action is unchanged. `IV_Sword` ships as `public/assets/iron-vanguard/sword-v001.glb`: 273,244 bytes, 16,590 triangles, one rigid PBR mesh, no textures. Each instance owns its geometry/materials so view rebuilds do not dispose cached source data.

Reproduce into a fresh output directory using Blender background mode and `scripts/iron-vanguard/blender/export_sword_runtime.py -- --output <directory>` with the canonical master as input. Then run `node scripts/iron-vanguard/pack_sword.mjs <directory>` and `npx @gltf-transform/cli meshopt <directory>/sword.glb public/assets/iron-vanguard/sword-v001.glb`. The exporter uses only the selected object in its dedicated active scene, validates the source action signature and writes grip calibration plus idle samples. The generated TypeScript contains 47 upper-body quaternion tracks at up to 30 fps over four seconds.

`ironVanguardSword.ts` converts the canonical sword geometry into the right hand's local space. A raw-orientation socket follows the rendered hand; the legacy independently animated sword offsets are bypassed. The idle/attack layer runs after lower-body movement and before aim. Arm IK keeps bone lengths fixed. Chest and shoulder turns lead a broad right-to-left slash, the elbow blends toward an outward/downward pole, the wrist retains its authored relationship to the forearm, and the offhand rises into a guard. Lunge uses a separate elbow path and a bounded 0.65-radian blade alignment correction toward facing. The calibration vector is measured from the shipping sword in hand space. All offsets ease back to the approved idle during recovery. Hammer-specific shoulder morphs are disabled only while the sword is equipped and restored on returning to the hammer. Shared factories cover bots, remotes and replays; local first-person uses the same prop and phase sampler. Combat clocks, collision, damage, cooldown and movement authority are unchanged.

Slash timing comes from `swordSlashSpeed` and recovery from `swordSlashReload` (defaults 0.22/0.60 seconds). Local `slashing` reaches contact at its existing half-duration hit; AI `swing_up`/`swing_down` reach contact at the end of swing-down. Each view maps that boundary to its own contact pose. The third-person slash follows a quadratic spatial arc with continuous velocity through contact; its progress curve accounts for the different local and bot clock intervals. Each recovery starts at its preceding pose. Lunge draws back for 0.04 seconds and extends over 0.10 seconds. The lower body enters a low split stance over 0.12 seconds, with the right foot ahead and the left trailing. Fixed-length leg IK preserves floor clearance. Both thrust and body pose hold for the actual gameplay flight rather than a fixed animation duration. On a hit, the actual interrupted arm pose blends into the existing cross slash over up to 0.065 seconds, including early contact during draw-back. The body rises over 0.38 seconds into idle or continued locomotion. A miss returns to guard over 0.22 seconds without a finishing slash. The cosmetic `swordLungeHit` outcome is set by the existing local/AI lunge completion owners and cleared at the next start. It travels through remote sync and replay recording/playback; replay change detection includes the flight and outcome flags. No extra slash action, damage event or cooldown is introduced. Local and roster flight supply elapsed time where available; other shared third-person instances accumulate visual flight time from the flag and delta time. The gameplay system still owns travel, collision, cooldown and hit timing. The review page supports both weapons with independent attack scrubbing and the existing eight-direction, look, sprint-speed, jump and slide controls.

`ironVanguardSwordView.ts` builds the first-person arms from the same cached body/skeleton. It retains only triangles whose vertices have majority arm/hand/finger skin weights, compacts the attributes, and omits hammer-only morph buffers. Geometry, materials and skeletons are disposed per view without changing cached assets. The camera-local shoulder/hand framing starts with a bent elbow and enough space for visible forward extension; the sword uses the actual rendered hand socket throughout the attack and lunge return. The first-person slash has its own cross-body path on the same phase clock. Camera-local rig translation and shoulder rotation carry the actual grip from right to left, with forward reach at contact and a diagonal blade angle. The forearm/wrist relationship stays fixed and the elbow stays bent. The blade moves continuously through impact and recovers on a lower path into guard. Only the first-person view consumes the rig translation returned by the pose function; the third-person path and gameplay roots are unchanged. Lunge keeps its separate extension and alignment path. Local animation bob moves the arms and weapon together. No additional GLB or source-master edit is required.

The **First-person** button (or `?weapon=sword&clip=idle&view=first`) reviews the arms with the local player's slash timing. Other views retain the bot phase mapping. Both use the same phase sampler and authored idle. Focused checks cover actual deformed palm/finger vertices remaining fixed in sword space, a bent elbow and stable wrist through the first-person sweep, right-to-left grip travel through center and depth, descending blade travel, a lower recovery path, speed-normalized contact, recovery continuity, full slash/lunge/pullback camera clearance, nonzero continuous cut velocity at contact, cross-body slash travel, forward thrust alignment, hit/miss transitions at early and late contact, low held stance, fixed leg lengths, return to movement, and interrupted-miss return without a slash recovery, disposal and unchanged impact/cooldown events. The lunge preview supplies explicit flight/release times so scrubbing matches playback; switching attacks or disabling attacks clears the preview state. Select **Lunge → contact slash** or **Lunge → miss**, with **Lunge travel time** from 0.12 to 0.80 seconds. `?weapon=sword&clip=idle&action=lunge` opens a moving lunge review; add `&view=first` for the camera view. The preview alone moves its character across the floor; production animation never translates the gameplay root. Regression checks also cover unchanged local/AI cooldowns, absence of a second hit, and remote/replay outcome propagation. Visual tuning can continue in this page; this implementation does not imply final art approval.
